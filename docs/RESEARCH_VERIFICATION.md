## 2. Critical Analysis of Claims

### Claim 1: "23-31% improvement in emotional awareness"
- **Source:** Studies 1-3 (Malchiodi 2020, Hass-Cohen & Carr 2008, Bakker et al. 2016)
- **Actual Finding Analysis:**
  - **Malchiodi (2020):** Book synthesis, not a specific study with this statistic
  - **Hass-Cohen & Carr (2008):** 2-page editorial claiming "31% enhancement" - NOT from empirical study
  - **Bakker et al. (2016):** Found 23% higher engagement, NOT 23% improvement in emotional awareness
- **HONEST ASSESSMENT:** ⚠️ **CLAIM IS OVERSTATED**
  - The 31% comes from an editorial, not empirical research
  - The 23% is about engagement rates, not emotional awareness
  - These numbers are being conflated and misrepresented
- **Corrected Claim:** "Visual apps show 23% higher engagement than text-only apps (Bakker et al., 2016)"
- **Confidence Level:** C (Weak - claim needs significant revision)

### Claim 2: "28% increase in therapy adherence"
- **Source:** Study 7 (Clough & Casey 2015)
- **Actual Finding:** Meta-analysis found "technological adjuncts increase adherence by 28%"
- **HONEST ASSESSMENT:** ✅ **CLAIM IS ACCURATE**
- **Context:** This is from a meta-analysis of 32 studies (N=4,892)
- **Limitation:** "Technological adjuncts" includes various tools, not just mood tracking
- **Confidence Level:** A (Strong - well-supported claim)

### Claim 3: "15-28% better treatment outcomes"
- **Source:** Study 7 (Clough & Casey 2015)
- **Actual Finding:** "15-20% better treatment outcomes with digital adjuncts"
- **HONEST ASSESSMENT:** ⚠️ **RANGE IS SLIGHTLY INFLATED**
- **Corrected Range:** Should be "15-20%" not "15-28%"
- **Note:** The 28% refers to adherence, not outcomes
- **Confidence Level:** B (Good - minor correction needed)

### Claim 4: "18-25% reduction in symptom severity"

## 3. Methodology Transparency

### How This Research Was Conducted

**Literature Search Process:**
1. Searched PubMed, Google Scholar, and institutional databases
2. Keywords: "mood tracking," "digital mental health," "visual therapy," "mHealth"
3. Inclusion criteria: Peer-reviewed, English, published 2008-2023
4. Preference for meta-analyses and systematic reviews

**Limitations of Our Literature Review:**

1. **Search Bias:** Focused on positive outcomes (publication bias exists in field)
2. **Recency Bias:** Prioritized recent studies (2012-2023), may miss foundational work
3. **Language Bias:** English-only sources
4. **Access Bias:** Some paywalled studies not fully reviewed
5. **Selection Bias:** Chose studies supporting our hypothesis

**What We Did NOT Do:**
- ❌ Did not conduct systematic review with PRISMA guidelines
- ❌ Did not include studies showing negative outcomes of digital tools
- ❌ Did not search non-English databases
- ❌ Did not include unpublished dissertations or grey literature
- ❌ Did not conduct meta-analysis ourselves

**Gaps in Evidence:**

1. **Visual Moodboards Specifically:** Limited research on visual moodboards as distinct from general art therapy
2. **Long-term Outcomes:** Most studies are 8-12 weeks; need 6-12 month data
3. **Therapist-Client Tools:** Most research is on consumer apps, not B2B tools
4. **Diverse Populations:** Limited data on effectiveness across age, ethnicity, diagnosis
5. **Cost-Effectiveness:** Few studies on ROI for therapists

**Conflicting Studies:**

We acknowledge these exist but did not cite them:
- Some studies show no benefit of digital mood tracking over standard care
- Concerns about over-reliance on technology reducing human connection
- Privacy concerns may outweigh benefits for some populations
- Digital divide may exclude vulnerable populations
